# Lasting Empires

A card-driven grand strategy game for tablets. Lead any of 148 realms from 1451, when Mehmed II takes the Ottoman throne, to 1750. One turn is one quarter of a year.

## Play

```
npm install
npm run build        # writes dist/index.html (standalone) and dist/lasting-empires.html
```

Open `dist/index.html` in any browser. The core menus (Military, Diplomacy, Internal, Economy, Research) open as a window with tabs: Cards, Missions (pick a person then a mission, or a mission then a person) and the menu's own pages. The game saves automatically in the browser, in one of three campaign slots (continue, replace or delete them from the title screen).

**Install it like an app.** On the GitHub Pages site, use the browser's *Install app* (Chrome/Edge) or *Share → Add to Home Screen* (Safari on iPad/iPhone). It then opens full screen from its own icon and works offline.

## How it plays

- **Everything is a card.** Five kinds: Military, Diplomacy, Internal, Economy, Research. You draw each at a yearly rate (1.0 base, fractions carry over), raised by buildings, research, cities and your monarch. Spend **nation points** to buy more: three are revealed, you keep one.
- **Three currencies.** Manpower (1 per troop or person hired), nation points (buy cards), ducats (troop upkeep, building salaries, goods bought abroad).
- **People** have a rarity: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Epic, Legendary. Rarity sets their skill and how strong their traits are. Roles: general, diplomat, administrator, merchant, researcher. Diplomats can become explorers, generals conquerors, administrators colonists — permanently.
- **Missions** start from a mission card plus a free person of the right role: conquest, raids, embassies, trade treaties, trade routes and trade posts, alliances, marriages, peace, research, buildings, development, new cities, voyages, colonies, conquistas.
- **Turns are quarters.** Income, manpower and cards come in each quarter; missions are measured in quarters (an embassy 2, a building 6–9, a religious mission 6–18 depending on your administrator). Each AI realm takes its yearly decisions in its own season. At the end of every turn a report shows your missions and the quarter's battles, each played out with its odds.
- **Armies stand somewhere.** Troops are stationed in the capital, in provinces with Barracks, or at an invasion base, and are shown on the map. Marching takes 0 quarters nearby (Toledo–Zaragoza), 1 across a land or a short sea (Asturias–Andalusia, Andalusia–Morocco, Paris–Lombardy), 2 far within a continent (Spain–Poland), 3 across the world. Only troops within a quarter's march can attack or defend a province. Spy Troop Positions (Military) and Spy Network (Diplomacy) show where a rival's armies stand.
- **Wounds and occupation.** A company wiped out is lost; one that survives below full strength rests in garrison (about a fifth back each quarter) and cannot fight until whole. Land taken in a war shows striped in both realms' colours until the peace confirms it.
- **Permanent cards.** Peace Envoy and Embassy stay in your hand: each journey costs ducats only, and the envoy is away for the trip there and back (one quarter each way to a nearby court). Every realm starts with a Peace Envoy.
- **Attack and defence.** When a rival marches on you, you see it a quarter ahead and choose the general and troops that meet them (they are held back for it, like any mission); then the battle plays itself while you watch (generals pick the tactics). Far targets need an invasion base first: a general marches out and builds a camp, which fights at half strength for one quarter while the enemy is warned; once it stands, nearby provinces are a quarter's march away.
- **Pick targets on the map.** An attack card (March Orders) offers "Select province on the map": every province you can conquer, raid or set a base against lights up. Hold it with a finger, or click it with a mouse, to choose.
- **Realm rank.** Small Kingdom (6 missions a year), Kingdom (5+ provinces, 9), Great Kingdom (13+, 12 and one viceroy), Empire (30+, 15 and three viceroys). A viceroy governs the provinces around a seat for you and takes 2 of your yearly missions. Who you send shapes it: a general builds forts and manpower, an administrator control and growth, a merchant buildings and ducats, a diplomat happiness, a researcher research. A big, unhappy viceroyalty, or a very able viceroy under a weak crown, may break away.
- **Civil wars.** Provinces below 25% happiness can rise. If the rebels beat your troops (local militia always help), they hold the province under their own banner — a faith league, a free people of a foreign culture, or a pretender when stability is very low — and unhappy provinces of the same cause next to them may join.
- **Coalitions.** Each conquest adds infamy, which fades over the years. A realm with high infamy and 8+ provinces finds its neighbours allying against it, and they declare war together when strong enough.
- **Later ages cost more.** Administration grows by age (×1 to ×1.85) and buildings cost 15% more in each new age.
- **Tutorial and test scenarios.** The first game can show a chancellor's notes step by step. The title screen's Test scenarios start Castile at war with Portugal in 1467 to try attacks, defence and invasion bases.
- **Constantinople, 1453.** The siege happens whoever you play. As Byzantium you have two years: hire Giustiniani, accept the union of the churches, find allies, raise troops — and even then it is a fight. As the Sultan it is very likely to fall.
- **The world.** 1,038 provinces and 148 realms on every continent, with three areas in equal detail: Europe (209 provinces), North Africa and the Middle East (149), East Asia (163). Any realm is playable: pick it on the world map. Each realm starts knowing its own part of the world.
- **Provinces** have a settlement (village → town → city → metropolis) and a countryside, each with its own building slots, plus population, development, control, one culture, one faith, a happiness level and a raw material.
- **Goods.** 13 raw materials (grain, fish, meat, fruit & vegetables, wood, iron, coal, leather, wool & cotton, dyes, saltpetre, gold & silver, spices & sugar) and 8 made goods (building materials, tools, weapons, gunpowder, cloth, books & art, naval stores, luxuries). Each province makes about 1 load a year of its raw material; farms, pastures, mines, fisheries, woodcutters and plantations double it on a matching good and make a little grain, meat, fish or wood anywhere. Workshops (2 loads a year, 2.5 with Guilds) and Manufactories (4) turn raw materials into the good you choose.
- **Needs.** The people eat (any mix of food; three kinds make them happier) and wear cloth; towns use building materials; farms and mines wear out tools; troops need weapons, guns need gunpowder, ships naval stores; temples, libraries and universities need books & art; the court needs luxuries. Shortages hurt: hunger and no cloth make people unhappy, without tools extraction halves, without weapons or naval stores the wounded don't recover, without books culture buildings work at half pace, without luxuries prestige falls. New buildings, settlements, companies and ships take goods from the warehouses; what is missing is bought at double price.
- **Trade.** Every deal takes a card from the Economy deck. An Import Contract or Export Contract (a merchant, one quarter) opens a route that buys or sells 1–4 loads a year of one good with a realm that makes it (or lacks it), every quarter, at 125–140% of the market price when buying and 70–85% when selling. A Trade Post Charter (a merchant, 2 quarters next door up to 6 overseas, then 2 ducats a year) makes every route with that realm buy at 90% and sell at 120%, and allows one more route. Trade treaties add 10%. Full warehouses sell the rest at home for half the price. The Economy menu's Trade tab shows every good's stock, flow, shortages and routes.
- **People menu.** A sixth kind of card. Courtiers take their first mission and become, for good, a churchman (Religious Mission, Mission to the Church), an artist (Work of Art) or a cultural leader (Royal Grace, Cultural Integration, Embrace a Culture); artists, churchmen and famous figures (Leonardo, Michelangelo, Sinan, Velázquez, Erasmus, Loyola…) can also be hired directly. Tabs: Cultures (each people's provinces, numbers and happiness; embrace up to 2 + 1 per 12 provinces), Religion (your church's leaders and their favour: blessed at 50+, censured at −40; Gift to the Church cards and churchman missions raise it, conversions and sacred art please it) and Arts.
- **Works of art.** Sacred paintings, royal portraits, epics, treatises and monuments. Quality comes from the outcome: Forgettable (+1 happiness for a year) up to Masterpiece (+6 happiness fading over 8 years, 6 ducats a year for 5 years, +8 prestige, 2 free cards of the work's kind, sometimes a famous name such as Amadís de Gaula or Las Meninas). Monuments last half as long again. Libraries, universities and Museums (Enlightened Government) show the best works; from the Age of Enlightenment visitors pay for them.
- **Card types.** Each deck has People cards (hire someone) and Action cards; Military also has Troops and Improvements. Troop cards are by kind: Levy Infantry, Hire Infantry, Hire Ranged, Hire Cavalry, Hire Artillery, each hiring the best unit of that kind you have researched (e.g. Crossbowmen → Arquebusiers → Musketeers → Line Infantry). Better kinds come from Forge recipes (Pikemen + Arquebusiers → Tercio). Military actions include March Orders, Conquista, Fortify and Spy Troop Positions; Research has Collaboration (a second scholar joins a study). Embassy and Peace Envoy are the permanent cards.
- **Cards.** Everything you do takes a card: trade contracts, works of art, gifts, attacks, buildings. No hand limit: identical cards stack (×7). Declaring war gives 3 March Orders; being attacked gives 2.
- **Map views.** Realms, culture, religion, raw materials (an icon on every province, with a legend), happiness and population.
- **Ages.** Crusade → Discovery → Reformation → Absolutism → Enlightenment. An age opens when the first realm reaches its research (Ocean Navigation, Absolutism, Enlightened Government) or event (a prince protecting a reformer). No ocean voyages before Discovery. The pioneer gains prestige; if it is you, also nation points, cards and +0.4 cards a year for 25 years. Tap the year to see what opens the next age.
- **Start dates.** 1451 (to 1750), 1600 (to 1780) or 1700 (to 1810), chosen in the realm picker. The later worlds have their own owners for all 1,038 provinces, 68 new realms (Dutch Republic, Mughals, Safavids, Qing, Marathas, Thirteen Colonies, Uzbek khanates, Ashanti, Dahomey...), Spain, Russia and Prussia under their new names, Protestant and colonial religions and cultures, vassals, the wars of the day, research already known, the government of the time and armies of the age. A sixth age, the Age of Revolutions, opens with the Rights of Man.
- **Beginner missions and unlocks.** Nine short beginner missions, on every continent, each teach one system (war, diplomacy, people, trade, buildings, the arts, research, troops, development) and hand over the cards they need. Completing them all unlocks the historic and wild missions; completing one of those unlocks the open game. "Unlock everything" at the bottom of the Missions screen skips this.
- **Mission mode.** Only the mission counts: winning it shows a celebration screen whatever your rank, failing it a defeat screen (try again, missions, or keep playing). Goals are checked every quarter.
- **Missions.** The long goal of the game is to complete every mission, in any order: the Missions screen counts how many you have completed (kept on this device across campaigns) and marks each one with the realm and year. Besides the open game, Missions offers historic challenges: a realm, a deadline and a few goals (Spain's Golden Age, The Conqueror, The Sea Road to India, Lion of St Mark, The Third Rome, A.E.I.O.U., The Treasure Fleets, The Gold of Mali, The Fifth Sun), and from 1600 (The Dutch Golden Age, Lion of the North, Akbar's Empire, Rule the Waves, The Long Turkish War) and 1700 (Liberté, Join or Die, An Army with a State, A Window on the West, Hindavi Swarajya, The High Qing), plus wild ones just for fun (Abubakari's Fleet, The Aztec Armada, Rome Reborn, The Swiss Navy, Ireland's Revenge, Prester John Rides, The Empire of Three Continents, Japan in Australia, The Pope Goes West, The Colonies Strike Back, The Mughal Grand Tour), or build your own (start year, realm, deadline, up to 6 goals). The Goals window shows where you stand on each, e.g. your navy against the leader's. Win or lose, the game goes on.
- **Government.** Feudal Monarchy (levies, richer farms), Absolute Monarchy, Parliamentary Monarchy, Enlightened Despotism and Republic, each with a few effects (Internal → Government). Changing it takes a Revolution card, drawn once you have researched the new form, and a civil war: the far provinces rise for the old order with troops of their own. Take their capital or break their army three times within 12 years; if your capital falls first, the revolution fails and costs treasure, cards and stability.
- **Hand limit.** How many cards you may hold grows with your realm: Small Kingdom 8 per deck and 22 in all, Kingdom 10/28, Great Kingdom 12/34, Empire 15/40 (permanent cards and debts do not count). Cards always arrive; if you are over, End Turn asks you to sell the extras for ½ nation point each. The Cards tab shows "9 of 12" for each deck.
- **Research.** 58 technologies, about 12 per age (4 each in Military, Economy and Statecraft). You can only study what belongs to the current age or earlier; later ages stay hidden until they open. Each technology shows its research points, how long your best free scholar would take (2–7 years is normal), what it unlocks and what it needs first. Filter by age and field in Research → Technologies.
- **Buildings** show their cost (ducats, construction materials, time, upkeep) and their benefit side by side. Only buildings you have researched are offered.
- **Monarchs.** When a ruler dies you choose one of three heirs. Each kind boosts different card rates, and some can act as a general, diplomat, merchant or researcher.
- **Cards tab.** Every menu's Cards tab shows what you hold, with filters (cards, people, and in Military troops, ships and equipment), and buttons to buy a pack (3 revealed, keep 1), archive cards you do not need now (they stop counting against your hand; restore or discard them later), forge and read the recipes.
- **Forge.** Two companies of a kind become one company about 1.8× as strong (and a free slot); troops that match a recipe become a better kind keeping 90% of their joint strength; equipment upgrades one troop. The recipe book lists only what your research allows.
- **Battles and history deck**: face-down tactics, branching historical events.
- **Navy.** Ship cards (galleys, caravels, carracks, galleasses, galleons, ships of the line) are fitted out by a general, who becomes an admiral for good. Attacks by sea need ships to carry the troops; enemy fleets intercept with sea battles (broadside, boarding, line of battle, weather gauge).
- **Peace.** Wars keep a score from provinces and battles. The winner spends it on reparations, provinces or vassalage.
- **Subjects.** Vassals pay a quarter of their income, personal unions 40%; both follow you to war and can later be integrated.
- **Happiness.** Each province has a culture and a faith. Happiness comes from faith, culture, arts, taxes, war weariness, food and festivals, and moves stability each year. Internal cards (patronage, religious missions, cultural integration, royal grace, festivals, charters, tolerance) act on it. Religious missions, cultural integration and royal grace go faster with a rarer administrator (legendary 2 years … common 6).
- **Overseas.** Explorers reveal regions of the map (Guinea, the Antilles, the Indies…). Colonists settle unclaimed native lands; conquerors take provinces of native empires. Colonial rivals and land neighbours settle the world too.
- **A world that reacts.** Other crowns offer alliances, marriages and vassalage; events answer how you rule: the new faith spreading, tax riots, plotting vassals, a genius painter, idle fleets, a golden age, petitions from foreign peoples, rival settlers.

## Code

- `src/data`: provinces (`provinces.ts`), generated map (`world.json`), nations, eras, troops and recipes, research, characters.
- `scripts/gen-world.ts`: builds `world.json` from Natural Earth coastlines (`npm run gen-world`) after editing provinces.
- `src/engine`: pure game rules (seeded RNG, no DOM). `turn.ts` runs a year; `ai.ts` runs rival nations.
- `src/ui`: map, cards, panels and modals.
- `scripts/sim.ts`: headless bot that plays full campaigns, for balance and crash testing.
